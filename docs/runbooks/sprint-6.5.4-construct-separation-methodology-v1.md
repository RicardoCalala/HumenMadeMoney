## Construct rule

Conceptual comprehension is the measured construct. Traditional computer proficiency, typing speed, spelling, grammar, writing style, keyboard-shortcut knowledge, and facility with technical interface conventions cannot determine conceptual comprehension. The same frozen instrument and presentation wording apply to groups A/B/C/D.

The frozen instrument already uses ordinary language, explicitly states the available choose-one response options, avoids hidden/dense interactions and jargon, and combines a short free restatement with recognition and application questions. There is no choose-all item in v1; if a future version adds one, it must say `choose all that apply` and be prospectively versioned/approved. Distractors and `not sure` options remain plausible and neutral. A scorer judges the meaning of free response, never its typing, spelling, grammar, length, or style.

## Permitted access/navigation assistance

Allowed methods include accessible digital or equivalent readable presentation, ordinary assistive technology, extra time, breaks, interface-operation explanation, neutral navigation instructions, verbatim repetition after technical loss, and physical navigation or selection assistance for an answer the participant independently chose. These methods are not a scoring disadvantage and require no diagnosis disclosure.

Prohibited assistance includes explaining answer meaning, leading rephrasing, eliminating choices, suggesting a response, highlighting a preferred answer, confirming correctness, interpreting the authority boundary, or rewriting the participant's meaning. If the requested support would change the construct, pause and fail closed.

## Separate observation and reporting

Use only bounded codes outside raw content:

- comprehension observation: the frozen semantic labels and candidate determination;
- interaction/access observation: `none`, `accessible_format`, `assistive_technology`, `breaks`, `extra_time`, or `navigation_only`;
- instrument observation: `none`, `wording_unclear`, `choice_structure_unclear`, `presentation_order_issue`, or `technical_presentation_issue`.

Do not infer a comprehension failure from an interaction/access code. Do not infer an interaction failure from a semantic label. An instrument problem is a version-level incident, not a participant fault. Reporting must show these three categories separately and must not include diagnosis, support narrative, or detailed background.
